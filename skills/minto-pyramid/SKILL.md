# Minto Pyramid

## Overview

Lead with the answer. Barbara Minto's Pyramid Principle (developed at McKinsey):
state your single conclusion first, support it with exactly three
mutually-exclusive reasons, then put evidence under each. Most drafts do the
opposite — they walk through context chronologically and reveal the point at the
end.

**Core principle:** The reader should know your conclusion by the end of the
first sentence. Everything after it exists only to support it.

## When to Use

Apply when a draft:

- Opens with background or context instead of the conclusion
- Buries the recommendation, ask, or decision in the last paragraph
- Reads as a chronological recap ("we did X, then Y, then Z…")
- Organizes into topical buckets ("what's working / what's not / thoughts") with the ask at the bottom
- Rambles, over-explains, or mixes the point with nice-to-have detail
- Needs to be exec-ready, skimmable, or compressed into a short email

Also when the writer asks "what's my actual point?" or "did I bury the lede?"

**Skip it for:** pure narrative (stories, post-mortems where sequence matters),
reference docs, or anything where suspense or chronology is deliberately the point.

## The Pyramid

```
              [ Single conclusion ]          one sentence, first
             /         |          \
        Reason 1    Reason 2    Reason 3     exactly three, MECE
        /  |  \      /  |  \      /  |  \
       evidence     evidence     evidence    facts — only what earns its place
```

MECE = **M**utually **E**xclusive (the reasons don't overlap) and
**C**ollectively **E**xhaustive (together they cover the case).

## The Five Moves

Apply in order. Each is a self-contained pass over the draft.

| # | Move | What it does |
|---|------|--------------|
| 1 | **Restructure** | Rebuild answer-first: conclusion → 3 reasons → evidence. Cut anything that doesn't sit under one reason. |
| 2 | **Buried-lede test** | Find the ONE sentence the draft is really building toward. If it isn't at the top, move it there. |
| 3 | **Three-reason audit** | Do the reasons overlap? Do they together cover the case? Is any one a detail pretending to be a reason? |
| 4 | **So-what pass** | Read each line and ask "so what?" Delete every line that can't answer it. |
| 5 | **Email version** | Compress to <150 words, same structure, evidence only where it earns its place. Keep the author's voice. |

## Guardrails

- **Lead with the ask.** Even topical buckets ("what's working / what's not /
  recommendation") strand the decision at the bottom — put it first.
- **Exactly three reasons.** Two feels thin; four means one isn't load-bearing or
  two overlap. Audit and merge until three load-bearing reasons remain.
- **Evidence earns its place.** Credit, backstory, and process detail usually
  fail the "so what?" test for a decision-maker. Cut them, or demote to one line.
- **Preserve voice.** Compress and reorder; don't rewrite the person into a
  robot. Keep their phrasing, hedges, and tone. Answer-first ≠ generic.
- **Surface, don't soften.** If the real conclusion is a hedge ("I'm leaning
  toward…"), keep the hedge — but still put it first.

## Example

**Before** (buries the lede — the ask is the last sentence):

> Hey all — quick update on the onboarding wizard. We rolled it out to half of
> signups two weeks ago. Activation is up ~4% but that's within noise.
> Time-to-first-project dropped 9→6 min. Support's seeing ~20 extra mobile
> tickets/week and step 2 has 30% drop-off. The pricing redesign is specced and
> might move conversion more. I'm mostly thinking we pause the wizard and move to
> pricing.

**After** (answer first):

> **My lean: pause the wizard rollout and move the team to pricing next cycle.**
> Three reasons:
> - **Mobile is hurting users** — ~20 extra support tickets/week, and step 2 drops 30%.
> - **The fix is expensive** — ~3 more weeks of eng to get mobile and step 2 right.
> - **Pricing is the better bet** — fully specced and likely to move conversion more.
>
> Upside so far is thin: time-to-first-project dropped 9→6 min, but activation is
> only up ~4% (within noise). Curious what people think.

The conclusion moved from the last sentence to the first. Three MECE reasons.
The so-what pass cut the rollout date and demoted the weak activation stat to one
honest caveat. Voice intact.

## The Five Prompts (verbatim — to run by hand)

The moves above as copy-paste prompts, if you'd rather drive them one at a time:

1. **Restructure** — "Rewrite this using the Minto Pyramid: open with my single
   main conclusion in one sentence, then exactly three supporting reasons, then
   the evidence under each. Cut anything that does not sit under one of the three."
2. **Buried-lede test** — "Read this draft and tell me the one conclusion it is
   actually building toward. If I only had one sentence to give my boss, what
   would it be? Then tell me where in the draft I hid it."
3. **Three-reason audit** — "Check my three reasons. Do they overlap, and
   together do they fully cover the case? Flag any that is a detail pretending to
   be a reason."
4. **So-what pass** — "Go line by line and ask so what after each one. Delete
   every line that cannot answer it. Show me what survives."
5. **Email version** — "Compress this into an email under 150 words in the same
   structure: answer, three reasons, evidence only where it earns its place. Keep
   my voice."
